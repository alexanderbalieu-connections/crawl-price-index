A sensitivity tool, not a forecast

Explore the range: what could a priced crawl-web be worth?

Almost nobody charges today, but the pay-per-crawl rails are one toggle away across much of the web. This is a sensitivity tool with no base case — it predicts nothing. It shows how a hypothetical annual market value depends entirely on assumptions you set. Every number here is illustrative.

The $0.50 we observe (Stack Overflow) is one posted quote, not a market rate. Cloudflare’s documented minimum is $0.001 and its own example is $0.01; observed effective rates cluster around $0.001–$0.05. Across tens of billions of daily crawls, a realistic blended clearing rate sits in that range — so the presets default there, and effective price softens as more of the web charges (crawlers substitute).

Each is an assumption set, not a forecast. Pick one, then move any slider.

Why this is separate

We keep the estimate away from the record.

The Crawl Price Index publishes what it observes: declared crawler policy across a ranked 50,000-domain frame, the prices sites actually post, and the advertised asks in a public machine-payment registry. This page does none of that. It multiplies numbers you choose by other numbers you choose, and it is kept on its own page so it can never be mistaken for the census.
