Coverage

Nearly half the web has no AI policy at all.

We read every domain in the Tranco top 50,000, not a sample. Most have never written a rule about AI crawlers — so there is no policy to enforce and nothing to price.

We report against both denominators. A block rate measured against every domain and one measured against domains that actually publish a policy are different numbers, and the difference is the finding.

Declared policy

Blocking is selective, not general.

Share of domains with a readable robots.txt that block each crawler by name. Sites are naming individual AI companies rather than closing the door, which is what makes a price possible at all.

What we will and will not tell you

An inference is never printed as an observation.

Most reporting on this subject blends the two. We separate them in the data structure, not just in the prose, and we publish the seams.

We observe

Prices actually quoted

A price enters the index only when a domain returned it to a request we made and logged. Every row carries the time it was observed.

We observe

Policy as written

What each domain declares about 18 named AI crawlers, re-read every week and stored per domain.

We disclose

Small panels, with their n

Price and payment findings come from a fixed 30-domain panel, published in full. Enforcement figures are quoted with their n and date on the coverage page, or not at all.

We refuse

A default that looks like a claim

Where we have not measured something, the index says so. No estimated fields, no modelled prices, and no commercial relationship with anyone we measure.

Access

We take no money from AI companies, publishers, CDNs or licensing platforms. Subscriptions are the only revenue, which is what keeps the index citable.
